  Current Grassland pipeline and data flow

  engine.rs:161), reachable only through the noclip mechanic; it is stamped into

  GrasslandLevel (generate_chunk.rs:295-306). Generation (grassland_level.rs)
  is a single per-column pass: flat ground at voxel y=0 (grass, or water where a
  28.6u-wavelength noise field exceeds 0.62), a solid VOXEL_LIGHT slab across 
  the entire top of the grid at 5.6u ("sky"), VOXEL_LIGHT voxels replacing turf 
  every 3.2u ("ground glow"), and trees on a hashed 9u lattice (~40% occupancy).
  The grid then flows through the identical indoor pipeline: warm-fluorescent
  BFS bake (calculate_lighting.rs), face-occlusion bake, greedy mesh + light
  volume (surface_mesh.rs), SVO build, and one of three renderers. FrameParams
  carries only camera/yaw/pitch/flashlight — the renderer is never told it is
  outdoors.

  World-generation and deterministic-continuity diagnosis

  Determinism itself is sound: every placement is a pure function of world
  coordinates and seed (lattice-sampled hashes identical to Level 0's
  cell_hash), so chunk order, worker vs. main thread, and streaming order cannot
  diverge. Two continuity defects exist regardless:

  - Canopy clipping: trunks jitter to within 1.0u of their 9u cell edge, but
  canopies have radius 1.6u; columns in the adjacent cell never consult the
  neighbor's tree, so canopies get sliced flat along invisible cell planes.
  - LOD instability of thin features: trunk radius 0.25u and the 3.2u glow tiles
  (gx < s) are sub-voxel at coarse LODs — trees and glow tiles pop in/out
  between LOD swaps rather than coarsening faithfully.

  Sky and daylight diagnosis

  (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q7) The sky is not an environment; it is an emissive ceiling 5.6u
  overhead — GRID_HEIGHT_UNITS = 5.8 is inherited verbatim from the indoor
  level. The white sky and the night ground are two halves of one mistake:

  - Emissive materials skip all lighting (color = albedo * 1.5, surface shader;
  FACE_INSTANCE_FLAG_EMISSIVE in the splat path), so the slab's underside
  renders blown-out white wherever it is inside fog onset (<15u — and it is
  always nearby, being 4u above eye level).
  - But fog still applies to emissive faces, so the same "sky" fades to
  baselineFogColor (0.15, 0.125, 0.055 — dark olive) by 50u. The horizon is
  therefore dark while the zenith is white — the inverted, "night with a white
  lid" reading.
  - Where there is no geometry at all, the framebuffer clear color is the sky:
  dark olive in the surface and CPU paths, pure black in the splat path
  (splat_webgl.rs:555).
  - The BFS actually bakes the ground bright: downward propagation has zero
  falloff, so every air column under the slab carries full 15 to the floor. The
  bake is not the problem — the renderer discards it (below). The turf glow
  voxels are therefore doubly wrong: redundant as light sources, and visible as
  glowing white tiles on the lawn since they replace grass at y=0 with an
  emissive material.
  - The bake is also unavoidably warm fluorescent ([15, 14, 11] hardcoded in
  calculate_lighting.rs) — daylight chroma is impossible, and the splat vertex
  shader further clamps irradiance to a warm floor (bakedWarm), so even a
  corrected bake would be forced amber.

  Terrain, water, vegetation, and scale diagnosis

  (Q9, Q10) Everything is one voxel thin and one frequency deep. Ground is a
  perfectly flat single layer — no relief, no macro terrain character. Lakes are
  threshold blobs of a single ~29u noise octave: no wetness macro-field, no
  shore band, no depth gradient; water occupies the same y=0 plane as grass and
  reads as blue carpet. Trees are the only vertical element, uniformly 3.6u tall
  from one fixed template, at near-orchard densities with no clusters, meadows,
  clearings, or landmarks. There is no scale hierarchy at all — the level has
  detail frequency but no identity frequency, and the 5.6u luminous ceiling
  erases any sense of open space regardless of what the terrain does.

  Indoor-rendering contamination diagnosis

  (Q5, Q8) Why the ground reads as night despite a full-strength bake, per stage
  in the surface path (shaders.rs:655-822):

  1. Irradiance crush: the light volume sample is blended 75% toward a
  dark-olive constant (mix(vec3(0.18,0.15,0.08), irradiance, 0.25)) — a Level 0
  mood decision.
  2. Interior ambient constants: the result is multiplied by yellow-green
  ambient tints (up: 0.35, 0.38, 0.22) sized so a fully lit interior stays dim;
  outdoor full bake yields ~0.14 ambient.
  3. No directional term: there is no sun; direct light comes only from the four
  nearest runtime point lights, and grassland has none, so directDiffuse = 0.
  4. Quantize + dark albedo: 5-band quantization drops ground light to the 0.25
  band; grass albedo (0.31, 0.60, 0.24) lands around 8-15% linear.
  5. Distance treatment: desaturation pulls toward warm dark gray (0.22, 0.18,
  0.12); fog (onset 15u, density 0.028, hard forced to 1.0 at 38-50u) blends to
  dark olive; height-based fog is darker near the floor — an indoor floor-gloom
  rule.
  6. Reinhard + gamma finishes the compression. The flashlight, grime
  modulation, and fluorescent flicker hash also run outdoors unchanged.

  The single shadow map serves one selected runtime point light
  (uShadowedLightIndex) — an indoor fixture concept; there is no sun-shadow
  path.

  Renderer-path consistency diagnosis

  (Q4, Q13) Three paths, three visual laws:

  hard 38-50u cut, clears to dark olive; fragment-shader material colors come

  Level 0 Anomaly Architecture — Diagnosis & Design Specification

  Part I — Diagnosis: why anomalies are currently visual tags, not phenomena

  Two generations coexist and the anomalies live in the dead one. MicrobiomeZone
  (cell.rs) — PillarField, Arch, RedRoom, Blackout, Holes, Atrium — is consumed
  only by the legacy level-1 generator (generate_chunk.rs:339-380), which the
  shipping Level 0 never runs. There, zones are per-5u-cell noise tags that swap
  cosmetic details during voxelization: PillarField plants corner/center posts
  in one cell, Arch curves a door top, RedRoom recolors boundary walls, Blackout
  deletes lights and lowers one cell's ceiling. Each is smaller than a single
  room, has no identity, no boundary condition, no interior plan, and no
  behavior. The current Level 0 (backrooms_level.rs + region_plan.rs) replaced
  them with real planning — circulation spines, assemblies, corruption — but its
  anomaly analogues are still thin: expanse ceiling bands with a column lattice
  (pillar field as ceiling weather), the red-room lighting corruption flag (one
  room's fixtures), abandoned assemblies (blackout as one dark room), and no
  arch vocabulary at all.

  Structural shortcomings to fix:

  1. No instance tier. Anomalies have no identity object. An expanse is wherever
  a noise field happens to exceed a threshold; it cannot have an entrance, an
  orientation, a size budget, an interior grammar, or state, and nothing can
  refer to it ("this expanse") across regions or frames.
  2. No cross-region footprint. RegionPlan is strictly 80u-local; only edge
  portals coordinate neighbors. Nothing today can plan a 300u phenomenon except
  accidental noise-blob continuity.
  3. No mutable tier anywhere. The entire pipeline is a pure function of (seed, 
  world position, level, lod) — which is exactly right for the immutable world,
  but there is no principled slot for deterministic state, so non-Euclidean
  behavior currently has nowhere to live except hacks.
  4. Materials cannot express gradients. One floor, one wall, one ceiling
  material; the palette is triplicated across voxel_mapper, build_octree, and
  the GLSL tables. Carpet depth, wetness, peeling wallpaper, damaged walls, and
  fluid are inexpressible.
  5. The light bake has one hardcoded color. calculate_lighting.rs emits
  warm-fluorescent from every VOXEL_LIGHT; per-fixture emission color exists
  only for runtime lights. Gradual red shift and cold glimmers can't reach the
  baked volume.
  6. No cue channel. No audio subsystem; the only exported non-geometry signals
  are runtime lights.

  The one great asset: chunks are fully evicted beyond the streaming footprint
  and regenerated from scratch on re-entry (ChunkStore::retain_keys, engine
  re-request). Collision, SVO, mesh, light volume, and face instances all derive
  from the regenerated grid, so a chunk that regenerates differently is
  automatically self-consistent across physics and every renderer. Remapping the
  world behind the player is therefore not a renderer trick to invent — it is a
  key extension to a regeneration mechanism that already exists.

  Part II — The hierarchy and the spatial-reality contract

  Six tiers, one contract:

  - Tier 0 — World seed. Unchanged (?seed=).
  - Tier 1 — Anomaly fields. Per family, a low-frequency world-space scalar
  field (200-500u wavelength, same construction as fabric_ceiling_band)
  nominating candidate territory. Pure (seed, x, z).
  - Tier 2 — Anomaly instances. Field maxima quantized to an anchor lattice
  (160u = 2x2 regions) yield instance records: instance_id = hash(seed, family, 
  anchor), plus footprint envelope (field-threshold distance function, so it can
  span many regions), orientation, bay/lattice parameters, entrance portals
  derived the same edge-shared way as v_edge_portal_z so all neighbors agree.
  Pure and immutable.
  - Tier 3 — Local architectural plan. RegionPlan gains anomaly overlays with
  explicit precedence: circulation spine → arch rooms → anomaly interiors →
  assemblies → fabric. Each overlay splits its plan into an immutable skeleton
  (bearing corridors, portals, glimmer routes, vestibules — everything
  navigation depends on) and mutable strata (infill between skeleton elements).
  - Tier 4 — Voxel realization. plan_column-style pure sampling, now also
  emitting material class, floor drop, fluid, fixture class and emission color.
  - Tier 5 — Encounter/remap state. The only mutable tier: per-instance epoch
  counters, owned by the application layer (engine), advanced exclusively by
  discrete semantic events (threshold/portal crossings). Deterministic replay:
  state is a fold over the ordered event sequence, so same seed + same traversal
  ⇒ same world, always.

  The spatial-reality contract: generation becomes a pure function of a
  RealityKey = (seed, level, chunk origin, LOD, and the epoch vector of
  instances overlapping that chunk). This key must travel intact through
  ChunkRequest → worker codec → generator, and the engine's existing
  stale-completion rejection (currently level-based) must also reject epoch
  mismatches. Because collision AABBs, the SVO atlas, surface meshes, the baked
  light volume, face instances, the minimap, and the blueprint debug renderer
  are all derived from the voxel grid, agreeing on the RealityKey is sufficient
  for physics/render/debug agreement — there is exactly one place where reality
  is decided. Two adjacent chunks must always be generated under identical epoch
  vectors; this is guaranteed by making epochs per-instance (world-scoped),
  never per-chunk, and it must be added to the chunks_tile_seamlessly-class of
  invariants (as a rule, not a test spec here). The halo-crop path
  (local_chunk_source) samples neighbor strips through the same key, so seams
  stay sealed.

  Never-change rules (global): no epoch may alter (a) any currently resident
  chunk — and residency here means the widest policy in play, the surface
  renderer's 9x9 visual footprint, not just the collision radius; (b) anything
  within a configurable safe radius of the player; (c) the player's occupied
  cell, ever; (d) any immutable-skeleton element; (e) any arch-room footprint
  plus margin. Epoch bumps are lazy: they take effect only when an affected
  chunk is next regenerated, which by construction happens beyond eviction
  distance — behind fog (hard fog wall at 38-50u ≈ eviction distance), behind
  the player, with zero pop and zero collision divergence. The single sanctioned
  sub-chunk exception is the red-room interior flip, which is legal only under
  its occlusion invariant (below).

  Part III — The four families

  1. Pillar expanses

  - Identity & footprint. Rare (one anchor candidate per ~160u cell passes a
  rarity hash; target one instance per 8-15 regions), footprint 100-400u via
  field threshold, so an expanse routinely spans many regions and can chain with
  a neighboring instance into a "possibly endless" belt. The instance stores
  lattice phase, bay dims (6.4-9.6u, snapped 0.4), column side (0.4-0.8), and

  - Initial regularity. Within an entry band (first 3-4 bays from any portal):
  perfect grid, uniform tall ceiling (4.6-5.4u), light panels on every second
  bay row aligned with the portal heading. This establishes bearing and rhythm
  before anything lies to the player.
  - Controlled distortion. A distortion budget grows with bay-graph distance
  from the nearest portal: column dropout, half-bay slips (the existing
  OffsetGrid vocabulary), doubled columns, ceiling half-steps — all pure
  functions of (instance, bay coords, budget). This part is Euclidean and
  stable.
  - Non-Euclidean model. Rows of paired columns form subtle gates every ~4 bays.
  Crossing a gate along a committed heading is the semantic event: it
  increments the instance's traversal epoch. The epoch re-hashes only the wake —
  bays beyond remap-min-distance behind the heading: infill panels appear
  between columns (architecturally plausible walls), alignments go asymmetric, a
  lane that was open returns blocked. Forward invariants: the skeleton
  contains, per epoch, a guaranteed open lane set from the player's current gate
  to every exit portal (computed on the immutable lattice graph,
  epoch-independent); infill may never intersect it, and portals never seal. The
  player can always keep a bearing; only return is unreliable.
  - Material identity. New "dry carpet" floor class: paler, desaturated, lower
  grime/flicker amplitude, wider light spacing — calmer than standard Level 0.
  - Evidence of shift. Gate pairs carry per-epoch asymmetries (one column
  thickened, a floor seam offset); the minimap, being derived from real resident
  geometry, shows the altered wake truthfully the moment it streams back in.

  2. Red rooms

  - Footprint & threshold. Promote the existing CorruptionProfile.red_room from
  "tinted fixtures" to an instance with a gradient annulus (5-9u) around a
  compact footprint (one assembly, 10-18u). The annulus is immutable and
  avoidable telegraphy: ring by ring — wallpaper material blends to
  peeled/crimson variants, carpet class steps through thick → wet → sticky (plus
  a one-voxel floor raise at the inner ring, which also is the spatial
  compression), ceiling steps down 0.2u per ring, fixture emission interpolates
  warm → red in both the baked volume (requires per-fixture bake color) and
  runtime lights (already per-fixture).
  - Disconnected topology, precisely. Outside: a normal doorway. Crossing the
  inner threshold is the semantic event that flips the instance state machine to
  closed: the interior plan re-hashes as a loop graph — a ring of segments
  where every "exit-looking" doorway maps to the next segment, and no portal in
  the interior plan voxelizes to the exterior. The exterior fabric independently
  voxelizes a sealed wall on its side of the same plane. Both sides sample the
  same instance state, so no chunk pair can ever disagree.
  - Observation invariant (the sub-chunk exception). The vestibule — everything
  visible from outside through the door, up to the mandatory first bend — is
  epoch-invariant. The flip commits only once the player's occupied cell is past
  minimum depth and the bend occludes the entrance. The engine then re-requests
  the instance's chunks under the new state; every voxel that differs is
  provably behind the bend and was never yet seen. No pop, no visible
  reconfiguration.
  - False return. Retracing across a loop-segment threshold backwards is itself
  an event: the segment behind re-hashes (epoch++), so the way back never
  replays. The forward loop order stays stable while observed.
  - Escape policy (explicit setting, never accidental). Modes: closed-loop 
  illusion with rare escape (default — each epoch, one segment may hash a
  crawl-height breach into a fabric crawlspace, probability = escape bias),
  always escapable, authored trap/game-over. Signals: carpet dries and light
  cools half a step in the true exit direction; the entrance gradient makes
  entry a choice, so the default is never an untelegraphed permanent trap.
  - Separated axes. Red material identity (wall/carpet variants), red emission
  (fixture color through bake + runtime), and red topology (the state machine)
  are three independent systems; the approach gradient uses the first two only.
  General Level 0 lighting stays untouched.

  3. Blackout expanses

  - Footprint & gradient. Instance tier like pillar expanses (120-300u).
  Approach: fixture density decays to zero over ~12u (the BFS bake dims
  naturally — the darkness is earned, not painted), ceiling forced through
  Compression into 2.4-2.6u, wall material shifts to a "damaged" class (rougher
  albedo, higher grime amplitude), floors develop one-voxel recessed basins that
  pool dark shallow fluid (reusing the walkable-water material contract;
  ankle-deep, visual-only, silent collision-wise).
  - Cues. Sparse emergency-glimmer fixtures (new fixture class: tiny, cool, very
  low intensity, 20-40u apart) sit on the immutable skeleton — a sparse
  survivor-route graph connecting entrance portals to exits. Following glimmers
  is therefore a genuinely meaningful strategy; a configurable fraction are
  decoys placed one segment off-skeleton. Audio does not exist yet: specify the
  cue-emitter contract now (per-chunk export like runtime lights: position, kind
  = buzz/drip/hum, radius) so a future audio driver consumes it without
  touching generation; until then glimmers carry the role alone.
  - Remap model. Same wake machinery as pillar expanses but with more aggressive
  eligibility, justified by darkness-as-occlusion: strata may re-hash when
  non-resident, or when beyond flashlight/fog reach (~24u) and baked light ≈ 0
  and off-skeleton. May never change: skeleton routes, glimmer positions,
  portals, anything within safe radius, the occupied chunk. At least one true
  route always persists because the skeleton is epoch-invariant; total-loss is
  only an explicit settings mode.
  - Structural distinction between the three mutable families: pillar expanses
  re-hash infill between a legible lattice (bearing preserved — the lie is
  behind you); blackouts re-hash the maze fabric itself around an invisible
  fixed skeleton (legibility survives only through cues); red rooms re-hash
  nothing in open fabric — they swap a closed interior graph through an explicit
  entry event.

  4. Archway rooms

  - Placement roles, not a zone. Two authored layouts: (a) transition rooms
  where the main corridor meets a secondary hall or — deliberately — any anomaly
  entrance portal (the airlock before pillar/blackout/red territory); (b)
  dead-end compositions capping TreeWithCulDeSacs stubs (that circulation style
  already exists) instead of bare terminations.
  - Composition. Rectangular 8-14u rooms; arches on a strict rhythm (openings
  1.6-2.0u wide, 2.4-3.2u apart) along the long walls — the arch profile math
  already exists in the legacy generator and gets promoted into the plan
  vocabulary as a first-class threshold type beside
  DoorWithLintel/OpenPortal/WidePortal. Every k-th arch is blind: a 1.2u-deep
  alcove, the shelter niche. Arches align enfilade on the long axis so
  sightlines compose repeated frames.
  - Materials. Pale bone-tinted wall class, unusually deep wet carpet class
  (darker floor + fluid pooling in alcove recesses), sparse warm pendants.
  - Stability contract. Arch rooms carry instance IDs but accept no epoch inputs
  by type — the remap eligibility predicate in every other family explicitly
  excludes arch-room footprints plus margin. They are the fixed landmarks the
  mutable world is measured against, and because they guard anomaly portals,
  they are also the last stable memory before topology goes wrong.
  - Circulation grammar. They attach like assemblies with two opposed portals
  (corridor side, anomaly side) and never sit on the main corridor centerline —
  corridor-first hierarchy is preserved.

  Part IV — Settings design

  All through the existing query_config discipline (parsed identically on main
  thread and workers, clamped, defaulted), organized as groups rather than the
  current flat knobs: anomaly (per-family rarity and size scale), remap
  (intensity → distortion-budget slope, min distance, safe radius, stability =
  epoch damping), gradients (annulus width, carpet-depth scale, fluid
  probability), redroom (escape bias, trap-mode enum), debug (blueprint overlay
  of instances/footprints/skeletons, epoch HUD readout, freeze-epochs switch for
  screenshots and visual tests). Epoch state must be serializable (session
  state, optionally URL-encodable) for replay and bug reports. The current
  LevelTuning fields keep their meaning as Level 0 fabric controls; anomaly
  controls are a sibling group, not more flat multipliers.

  Part V — Priority-ranked redesign list

  1. Spatial-reality contract: RealityKey + epoch vector through ChunkRequest,
  worker codec, stale rejection, and LOD refinement equality (ports.rs,
  worker_source.rs, chunk_codec.rs, engine.rs pending map) — everything else
  depends on it.
  2. Anomaly field + instance layer in the core beside region_plan, with overlay
  precedence in plan_column and the skeleton/mutable-strata split.
  3. Material palette unification + new classes (carpet depths, wall variants,
  fluid, damaged, pale) — collapses the voxel_mapper/build_octree/GLSL
  triplication first (same prerequisite the Grassland report identified).
  4. Bake parameterization: per-fixture emission color into calculate_lighting —
  the red gradient and glimmers are blocked without it.
  5. Engine event system: threshold-crossing detection, per-instance epoch
  store, targeted occluded re-request (surgical reuse of the noclip purge
  machinery).
  6. Arch vocabulary + transition/dead-end placement in region_plan.
  7. Settings groups + debug overlays (query_config, blueprint_renderer, HUD).
  8. Cue-emitter export contract in the chunk payload for the future audio
  driver.

  Part VI — Risks to avoid

  - Per-frame or frame-timing randomness. The engine's noclip xorshift is
  stepped per-frame and is not replay-deterministic; anomaly state must never
  touch it. Epochs advance only on discrete traversal events.
  - Renderer-only illusions. Any remap that bypasses regeneration guarantees
  collision/SVO/mesh disagreement, because all three derive from the one grid —
  that derivation is the safety mechanism; never route around it.
  - Per-chunk random changes. Epochs are per-instance and world-scoped; two
  neighbors generated under different epoch vectors would seam-tear. The key
  must make that impossible by construction.
  - LOD epoch mixing. A coarse resident chunk and its fine refinement must carry
  the same epoch; extend the existing "late coarse never downgrades fine" rule
  to epoch equality.
  - Visible pops. Remap eligibility must key off the widest residency (surface
  renderer's 9x9 visual footprint) and the fog wall, not the 3x3 collision
  radius.
  - Untelegraphed traps. Red-room closure without the gradient annulus, or
  blackout total-loss outside an explicit mode, is a design failure — policy is
  a setting, signaled in-world.
  - Category errors the current code already made once: red as merely a color
  (materials ≠ emission ≠ topology), blackout as merely deleted fixtures (it is
  ceilings, materials, floors, sound, and a cue skeleton), arches as merely
  rounded door cutouts (they are whole stabilizing rooms), pillar fields as
  merely repeated center posts (they are a legible lattice whose wake lies).
  - Losing architectural plausibility. Every impossible event must be expressed
  through possible-looking construction — infill panels between columns, a wall
  where a door "must have been" — never through geometry that visibly violates
  its own material logic. The contradiction is the player's memory versus the
  building, not the building versus itself.
